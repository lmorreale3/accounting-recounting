Looking ahead: Leggio and Conti

Demonstration · Workshop 4

The series closes with a demonstration of Leggio and Conti, two applications that bring the capacities of the tools we use throughout the workshops into one working environment.

  • Leggio combines transcription, Handwritten Text Recognition (Kraken models trained as in eScriptorium), large language model readings and corrections, page-by-page progress tracking, and data export (including ALTO for eScriptorium).
  • Conti reads Leggio's transcriptions of the account books to explore and visualize their data: sums in florins and lire, soldi and denari, dates converted from Florentine style, cross-references to other books, and kinds of entry.

Leggio and Conti are shown as a demonstration only; participants will not need to install or use them. They make most sense once you have worked directly with each of the underlying tools—FromThePage, eScriptorium and HTR models—so that you can see what each contributes and where the combination helps.

The manuscripts

Oversize Ms. Codex 1321

Ledger of Medici accounts

Italy, 1465–1494. Kept by Alamanno de' Medici: household income and expenses, loans to Florentine families such as the Risaliti and Salviati, and small private sales of textiles. Single-entry bookkeeping in three columns (transaction number, description, amount).

Catalogue record & images (Colenda)

Ms. Codex 1508

Ledger of Medici debtors and creditors and accounts

Florence, 1475–1483. 147 paper leaves, largely in Alamanno di Bernardo de' Medici's own hand, with other scribes. Records dealings with workers, tenants and employees such as millers and bakers, his son Bernardo, and the management of one of his farms.

Catalogue record & images (Colenda)

Both volumes are part of the Gondi-Medici Business Records, Kislak Center for Special Collections, Rare Books and Manuscripts.
