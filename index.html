About the series

The financial account books produced in fifteenth-century Italy offer a remarkably granular look into the social, financial, and material lives of their creators. Yet these dense primary sources, written in challenging scripts like mercantesca, frequently present high barriers to entry for researchers and students alike.

“Accounting and Recounting: The Daily Life of a Medici” bridges traditional manuscript studies with modern digital humanities practices. The series guides participants through reading and transcribing the sources, then segmenting and training Handwritten Text Recognition (HTR) models on the Medici account books held at Penn.

Grounded in the archival holdings of the Penn Libraries, specifically MS 1321 and MS 1508, participants will explore how routine financial record-keeping acts as a form of social narrative, balancing quantitative data extraction with qualitative microhistorical analysis.

Participants should plan to attend all four workshops; each workshop will require 2–3 hours of participant time.
